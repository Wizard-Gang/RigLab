# Architecture

RigLab authors skeletons and motion, not character artwork. Its rig model and its clock are Hexframe's: RigLab vendors Hexframe's rig engine byte-for-byte and builds on it, so a skeleton or pose authored here is placed by exactly the code Hexframe draws with, and an animation plays through exactly the sampler Hexframe plays its clips with.

## The Hexframe rig model

`vendor/hexframe/` mirrors these Hexframe repository paths at the commit pinned in `vendor/hexframe/lock.json`:

- `src/rig/` — rig contract types, `createRig` (single root, no cycles, every bone reached), `hierarchyOrder`, `forwardKinematics`, `inBone`, the 13-pose clip type and `sampleClip`, depth profiles and paint order.

RigLab vendors no Hexframe character, clip or part art. The chibi is RigLab's own, and `tests/chibi/` pins it to Hexframe's 11-bone tree and chains, and to its bend sides except the front arm's, which folds outward (see Chibi rig).

The conventions that follow from that code:

- **Space.** SVG units, x right, y down, rotation clockwise-positive. The chibi stands 91 units tall with its feet at y = 0.
- **Parent-relative transforms.** Each bone's `offset` is its joint position in its parent's frame; the root's offset is in rig space. `tip`, when present, is the bone's end point in its own frame.
- **Forward kinematics.** A bone's world rotation is its parent's world rotation plus its local rotation (degrees in a pose, radians once placed). Its world position is the parent's position plus the parent-rotated sum of its offset and any local translation.
- **Proportions.** Limb and segment lengths are the lengths of child offsets and tips. Poses translate only the root, so every limb keeps its rest length on every frame; `tests/rig/hexframe-rig.test.ts` proves that for every frame the engine plays, for every kind.
- **Clips.** A Hexframe clip is 13 evenly spaced poses (`POSE_INTERVALS` = 12) over `duration` frames of Hexframe's fixed 60 Hz simulation clock, eased `linear` or `smoothstep` between neighbours.

Nothing under `vendor/` is edited in place. `npm run vendor:hexframe -- <hexframe-checkout> <commit>` re-vendors from a commit's Git objects and rewrites the lock; `npm run check:vendor` (part of `npm run check`) fails on any edited, missing or unpinned file.

## RigLab formats

`src/format/` defines RigLab's portable, versioned contract. Every validator is closed: an unknown field, bone or value fails with the JSON path it names.

- **`riglab.rig` version 1** keeps Hexframe's rig contract fields (`space`, `root`, and bones with `name`, `parent`, `offset`, `tip`, `slot`, `artHeight` and `hand`) and adds a `format`/`version` header, a `name`, nullable per-bone angle `limits` (`min`/`max` local degrees) and two-bone `chains`. A chain's `lower` bone hangs off its `upper` bone: the upper segment is `lower.offset`, the lower segment is `lower.tip`, and `bend` (`1` or `-1`) is the sign of `lower`'s local rotation away from straight. The space must be SVG units, x right, y down and rotation clockwise-positive, because the FK math assumes it. `depthProfiles` is optional consumer layering data in Hexframe's shape. Every valid RigLab rig resolves through Hexframe's own `createRig`.
- **`riglab.anim` version 3** names its rig, its `class` and its `kind`, and carries `fps`, `length`, `loop` and its keys. The class is `unarmed` or the name of a library weapon, which every key holds, and no weapon may be named `unarmed`. The animation's `name` must be `<class>-<kind>`, such as `unarmed-idle` or `sword-attack`, so a library holds at most one animation of each class and kind. The kind fixes the rest (see Engine timing): `fps` is always 60, and the keys sit exactly at the kind's frames, so validation refuses any other rate, length, looping or key frame. Each key holds a full Hexframe pose, with a rotation for every bone and `x`/`y` on the root only, so no pose can stretch a limb. Its `interpolation` (`hold`, `linear` or `smoothstep`) shapes the transition to the next key.
- **`riglab.weapon` version 1** (`src/format/weapon.ts`) is a weapon rig: a `name` and one or more rigid `bodies`, each with a `name`, an art `slot`, an `axis` (its butt and tip in its own frame, for its line and turn handle) and one or two `grips`. A grip is a fixed point `at` in the body's frame, held by the end of a hand `chain`, and a chain grips at most once per weapon. A body with two grips is free: a key places it, and both hands solve to its grips. A body with one grip rides that hand: its grip also carries an `angle`, the body's rotation relative to the chain's lower bone, so the body follows the hand and nothing else places it. Dual daggers are two one-grip bodies; a two-handed sword is one free body.
- **`riglab.palette` version 1** (`src/format/palette.ts`) is an art set's look as data: `classes` maps each class token its parts use to a `fill`, `stroke` (`#rrggbb` or `none`), `strokeWidth`, `opacity` and `linecap`, in cascade order, and `pixel` marks a pixel-art set, which draws no strokes and renders with crisp edges. `paletteCss` turns a validated palette into CSS scoped to `.art-set--<set>`, so no unvalidated text reaches a stylesheet.
- **`riglab.hold` version 1** (`src/format/hold.ts`) is where a weapon's free bodies first sit when the rig, standing at rest, takes it: the weapon's `name` and one placement per free body, checked the way a key's `bodies` are.
- **Key constraints.** A key may carry `pins`, each a chain end held at a world point, such as a planted foot. Every chain must exist in the rig, and a chain the class weapon grips cannot be pinned. A key of a class whose weapon has free bodies carries `bodies`, placing each of them with `body`, `x`, `y` and `rotation`; every other key carries none, because the daggers ride the hands and an unarmed key holds nothing. Classes replaced the per-key `weapon` of version 2, which replaced the older, ad hoc `prop`, and a key that still holds either is refused at its path, such as `$.keys[2]: has unknown field 'prop'`. RigLab reads only version 3: both libraries' version 2 animations were migrated to `unarmed-<kind>`.
- **`sampleAnimation(animation, frame)`** interpolates key poses deterministically. A looping animation samples `frame mod length`, and its last key runs to key 0 at `length`. A non-looping animation clamps to `[0, length]` and holds its last key. Blending uses Hexframe's `start + (end - start) * progress`.
- **`samplePose(rig, animation, frame, weapons)`** (in `src/solve/constraints.ts`) samples the key poses, then holds the frame's constraints, re-solving every pinned or gripped chain by IK: pins, then the grips of the class weapon's free bodies. Bodies that ride a hand need no solve; `bodyTransforms` (in `src/solve/weapons.ts`) places them on the solved hands. Pins and free bodies move between keys with the same segment and easing as the pose, and a pin holds still when only the key in force has it. An animation with no constraints samples exactly as `sampleAnimation` does. This is how each of Hexframe's 13 poses is made.

## Engine timing

Every animation runs on Hexframe's engine timing (`src/format/timing.ts`, `src/solve/engine.ts`), so what RigLab previews, bakes and exports is what Hexframe plays.

- **Rate.** Hexframe advances on a fixed 60 Hz simulation clock (`FPS = 60` in its combat constants). One RigLab frame is one Hexframe tick, so `ENGINE_FPS` is 60 and an animation's `length` is its Hexframe clip `duration`. Nothing is converted or rounded on export.
- **Kinds.** An animation's kind fixes its length, looping and keys. The lengths come from Hexframe's own clips, rounded to multiples of 12 so each of the 13 poses falls on a whole frame. Keys are evenly spaced on that pose grid from frame 0; a looping animation's last key runs back to its first at `length`.

| Kind | Keys at frames | Length | Plays | From Hexframe's |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `idle` | 0, 15, 30, 45 | 60 | Loops | `labIdle` (60, looping) |
| `walk` | 0, 12, 24, 36 | 48 | Loops | `labWalk` (48, looping) |
| `jump` | 0, 12, 24, 36 | 36 | Once | `jump` (38) |
| `attack` | 0, 8, 16, 24 | 24 | Once | `labStrike` (20) |
| `hit` | 0, 12, 24 | 24 | Once | `labStagger` (24) |

- **Playback.** `engineOf(rig, animation)` makes the animation's Hexframe clip: `samplePose` at each of the 13 pose frames (`i × length / 12`), with `linear` easing between poses, as most of Hexframe's own clips use. `playAt(engine, frame)` returns that pose exactly on a pose frame, and Hexframe's vendored `sampleClip` between two of them. Pins and free weapon bodies move linearly between the same two poses. A key's interpolation therefore shapes the poses between keys, and Hexframe's sampler moves between those poses: a `hold` keeps its pose until the pose before the next key, then moves to it over one pose interval, and a `smoothstep` eases across the poses. `engineOf` moves each pose's rotations by whole turns, so each is within half a turn of the same bone in the pose before. Hexframe's sampler interpolates the raw numbers, and the two-bone solver can land a held arm one turn away from the pose before, which would spin the arm a full circle. Keys still interpolate their raw numbers, so a key can swing a bone more than half a turn to the next. The canvas, playback, onion skins, the baked frames and the Hexframe clip export all play through `playAt`, and `tests/solve/engine.test.ts` shows that Hexframe's sampler on the exported clip matches it on every frame of every kind.
- **Why.** Before this, animations had any rate and any key frames. 24 frames at 14 fps is 102.86 Hexframe frames, which export had to round, and keys between Hexframe's poses played differently in RigLab and in Hexframe. Engine timing makes that impossible: the rate is Hexframe's, every length is a whole number of ticks, and every key is one of Hexframe's poses.

## Editor

The editor is a pure state machine in `src/editor/state.ts`. Its model is the library folder itself (`src/editor/model.ts`): the folder's `riglab.rig`, every `riglab.weapon` in config order with its hold, whether or not a key holds it, and the `riglab.anim` animations. `libraryModel` builds it from the loaded library, and `validateModel` checks it as a whole, with every nested message re-rooted at its path in the model, such as `$.weapons[1].hold.bodies`. Model edits go through a bounded snapshot history (`src/editor/history.ts`, 100 steps); selection, mode, frame, view, grid and snap stay outside it. The model is never a file of its own. The editor tracks it as last loaded or saved, so it is unsaved exactly when the present snapshot differs. Revert (`revertState` in `src/editor/core.ts`) is enabled only then and asks first. It makes that saved model the present again with a fresh history, so undo cannot bring the discarded changes back. A saved library that lacks a class or kind gets it started again, as on open. The mode, the animation, the frame, the view and any selection the saved model still has stay.

- **Modes.** Rig setup shows the rest pose, and edits the rig and the active class's weapon. Animate plays the active animation at 60 fps, scrubs and steps it, and, paused, poses its key at the current frame; the editor opens in Animate. Export plays it too, beside the export panel. Playing is part of the editor's state (`playing`, set by `setPlaying`): Space and the transport's Play toggle it, stepping or seeking pauses it, a one-shot animation stops at its end, and Rig setup stops it. While it plays the canvas hides its drag handles, the pose inspector's fields only show, and the reducer refuses a pose edit. Switching modes keeps the frame.
- **Selection.** One selection: a bone, an animation, a key, or a body or grip of the class weapon in Rig setup. Delete removes only the selected joint and its subtree, in Rig setup. Animations and keys stay: the library holds one animation of each class and kind, and an animation keeps every key its kind gives it. Escape clears the selection.
- **Animations.** The Animations panel puts a class switcher (Unarmed, then each weapon in config order) over the active class's five kinds, each with its keys, length and looping. The active class is the active animation's. Choosing another class (`setClass`) keeps the kind, so `sword-attack` goes to `spear-attack`, and keeps the frame and a selected key or bone; a selected animation becomes the new one, and a class without that kind is refused. The grid of classes and kinds fixes the animations, so the panel adds, copies and deletes none, and the inspector shows an animation's name without a rename field. When the editor opens (`openState`), `fillClasses` (`src/editor/classes.ts`) starts every class and kind the library lacks, after the animations it has, in config and kind order, as unsaved changes: the status says how many, and Save writes them. It never changes an existing animation, so filling a full library does nothing. A missing unarmed kind starts at the rest pose. `startClassAnimation` starts an armed one from the unarmed animation of its kind: every key keeps its frame, interpolation and pose, and its pins on chains the weapon does not grip. A free body sits at the weapon's hold carried with the torso (`carryReady`), and both arms re-solve to its grips, so the legs, pelvis, torso and head stay the unarmed ones. A grip a hand cannot reach is noted, never stretched. Dagger keys keep the unarmed arms, because the daggers ride the hands.
- **Timeline.** In Animate and Export the timeline (`src/editor/timeline.ts`, `src/app/Timeline.tsx`) shows the active animation's frames, ticked at Hexframe's 13 poses, its keys and a playhead, and the transport names its class and kind. Its only edit sets a key's transition to hold, linear or smooth, as one undo step; the kind fixes the keys, rate, length and looping, and the timeline shows them. Arrows step frames, Shift+arrows step keys and Space plays. Onion skins draw the previous and next keys as faint skeletons. Preview and playback show `playAt`, so what plays is exactly what Hexframe plays.
- **Rig setup.** Only Rig setup changes anatomy: the reducer refuses a rig edit in any other mode. `src/editor/rigEdits.ts` moves a joint (dragged on the canvas, snapped in rest space and stored as an offset in its parent's frame, or typed), sets tips, slots and names, adds a child joint, deletes a joint with its subtree, sets angle limits and adds, removes or re-bends chains. Every edit must leave the whole model valid, so an edit that would remove a chain a library weapon grips, such as deleting `arm-front`, is refused with the weapon and chain named. Renaming a bone renames it in parents, chains, depth profiles and every animation key; deleting one drops it from chains and keys, and drops depth profiles that would name a deleted bone. Pose values never change.
- **Saving.** Save posts the rig, every library weapon with its hold, and the animations to the dev server's `POST /__riglab/library` (`src/editor/librarySave.ts`, `src/app/useLibrarySave.ts`), which writes the library folder (see Library folders). On success the editor marks them saved and keeps its history, mode, animation and frame. The status names the folder and how many files changed. A refusal shows the first error and how many more there are. Without the dev server, the status says Save needs `npm start`. When another page saves (`riglab:library-saved` with a save ID that is not the editor's), a clean editor reloads, and an editor with unsaved changes warns instead. Events that arrive while the editor's own save is in flight wait for its answer, so the editor never mistakes its own save for another's. `src/editor/bundleFiles.ts` writes export bundles to a folder or a zip, and downloads the Hexframe rig and clip.
- **View.** `src/editor/view.ts` keeps zoom and pan in rig units. The wheel zooms about the cursor, dragging empty canvas pans, and zoom 1 fits the rig's viewBox plus a margin. Grid lines sit on multiples of the spacing, and minor lines drop out when they would crowd the screen. A press on the canvas starts no browser text selection, which WebKit would paint over the canvas and panels. The editor and the wardrobe set `user-select: none` outside text fields, and a press on the canvas cancels its default action, blurring a focused field so that it still commits. Canvas handles are named with `aria-label` rather than an SVG `<title>`, so no tooltip pops up while posing.
- **Panels.** The left panel holds the bone hierarchy and the animations; the right panel is the mode's inspector. With nothing selected it says what to do next: Rig setup asks for a joint or a weapon part, Animate for a joint on a key, and Export lists what it writes. Each fits its width: long bone and animation names end in an ellipsis and show in full as a tooltip, labels, code names and button labels never wrap mid-word, and bundle paths break only after a `/`. Each panel's header has a collapse button that shrinks it to a thin rail with its name, and the canvas takes the width; the rail's button opens it again. The hierarchy section folds behind a toggle, so the class switcher stays in view. Which panels are collapsed and whether the hierarchy is folded (`src/editor/panels.ts`) are per-viewer conveniences kept in browser storage when the browser allows it, never in the model or its history. On screens 860 px wide or less the panels stack under the canvas, and a collapsed one keeps only its header row.
- **Shortcuts.** `src/editor/shortcuts.ts` maps Mod (⌘ on macOS, Ctrl elsewhere) plus S (Save), Z, Shift+Z and Y, and Delete and Escape. Save is the only file command, because the library folder is the editor's only source. Keys typed into a field stay with the field.

## Posing and constraints

`src/solve/` poses the rig by rotation alone, so every limb keeps its length by construction: no solve can translate a bone other than the root. Its priorities are fixed lengths and connectivity first, then angle limits and the chain's bend side, then grid snap of the drag target where the limb can reach it.

- **FK posing.** Dragging a joint swings its parent bone about the parent's joint, so the joint points at the cursor. Dragging the end of a tipped bone that is not a chain end aims that bone itself. Dragging the root translates it. Rotation snaps to the chosen increment (free, 1°, 5°, 15° or 45°) and is then held inside the bone's limits. Every new rotation is the equivalent nearest the current one, so a keyed rotation never spins a full turn on its way between keys.
- **Two-bone IK.** Dragging a chain's end (the lower bone's tip) solves the chain analytically with the law of cosines. Both segment lengths are kept and the bend stays on the chain's `bend` side. If an angle limit caps the bend, the capped chain is aimed so that its end still points at the target. A target beyond full extension, or inside the folded reach, leaves the limb at its closest reach and reports the shortfall.
- **Grid snap.** The drag target snaps to the grid when the limb can reach the grid point. Otherwise the exact pointer position is used if it is reachable, and the status bar says so.

- **Pins and grips.** Pin end holds a chain's end where it is. After any body move every pin and grip is re-solved, so lowering the pelvis with both feet pinned bends the knees instead of sinking the feet. Dragging a pinned end moves its pin. A pin or grip that cannot be held is noted in the status bar's normal tone with how far its chain end is from it, such as `leg-front is 1.20 off its pin`, and the limb is never stretched. A hand or foot dragged past its reach straightens the limb toward the pointer, and stopping at a limit is the same: the pose is the one asked for, so it has no note. The error tone is for refused edits.
- **Weapons.** An animation's class holds its weapon on every key, through pose drops, timeline edits and undo. Its hands solve to the free bodies' grips by rotation alone, inside angle limits and on each chain's bend side. A grip a hand cannot reach is noted with its chain, weapon and grip, such as `arm-front is 15.25 off the staff's far grip`, and the limb stays at its closest reach. A hand that holds the weapon cannot be pinned.
- **Weapon setup.** With an armed class active, Rig setup draws the chibi at rest holding the class weapon at its hold, with both hands solved to its grips (`holdAtRest`, `src/editor/weaponEdits.ts`). The hierarchy lists the weapon's bodies and their grips after the bones, and selecting one opens the weapon inspector (`src/app/WeaponSetup.tsx`): a free body's hold (`x`, `y` and `rotation`), the axis butt and tip, and each grip's `at`, with its `angle` on a one-grip body. The canvas edits the same values: a free body's move and turn handles set the hold, a grip point or an axis end drags in the body's own frame (`pointInBody`), and a riding body's turn handle, just past its tip, sets its grip angle (`ridingAngle`). Each weapon edit (`setHold`, `setGrip`, `setGripAngle`, `setAxis`) is one undo step, only in Rig setup, and must leave the whole model valid, so two grips on one point are refused. A grip the hands cannot reach at rest is noted, and the arm stays at its closest reach. Keys place bodies and the hands solve to the grips, so a moved grip moves the hands on every key of the class, and the inspector says so; a moved hold places the weapon at rest, in the wardrobe and in new animations, and existing keys keep their placements. Save writes `weapon.json` and `hold.json` through the library save.
- **Weapon handles.** In Animate, paused, a free body of the class weapon has a move handle at its grips' midpoint and a turn handle at its tip, and a drag places it on the key at the current frame. Dragging a hand that grips it carries it, and bodies that ride a hand follow it. With Art off each body draws as a line along its axis.

In Animate, paused, a drop writes the solved pose, its pins and its body placements into the active animation's key at the current frame. A frame between keys has no key, so a drop there is refused with the frames that do. The pose inspector types a rotation, or the root's position, and toggles pins the same way. Each drop is one undo step, and poses change only in Animate, while it is paused.

## Export bundles

Export mode builds a `riglab-export/` bundle (`src/export/bundle.ts`) for consumers. Some will evaluate keys and constraints themselves; others only draw solved transforms. Export only writes: RigLab never reads a bundle or a zip back, so the library folder is the only way a rig or motion enters the editor.

- `manifest.json` (`riglab.export`, version 1) names the generator and lists the rig, every animation, the weapons they hold in config order and any baked files, each with its SHA-256. File names follow from validated names: `<rig>.rig.json`, `animations/<name>.anim.json`, `weapons/<name>.weapon.json` and `baked/<name>.baked.json`. A bundle whose animations hold no weapon has no `weapons` list, as before.
- A baked file (`riglab.baked`, version 1, `src/export/bake.ts`) holds, for every Hexframe tick (0 to `length - 1` when looping, 0 to `length` otherwise), each bone's world `[x, y, rotation°]` in rig order, computed with `playAt` and Hexframe's FK. On frames that hold a weapon, its `weapons` entry gives each body's world `[x, y, rotation°]` by name, riding bodies included; frames without one are `null`, and a bake with no weapon has no `weapons` field.
- Export writes the bundle into a `riglab-export` folder through the File System Access API when the browser offers it. It can also download `riglab-export.zip`, built by a dependency-free ZIP writer (`src/export/zip.ts`) whose stored entries and fixed timestamps make the same bundle produce the same bytes.
- Tests check what export writes, directly (`tests/export/written.ts`): the manifest lists every other file, each entry's SHA-256 matches its bytes, the rig, animation and weapon files parse back to the editor's own, and each baked file equals a fresh bake of them. The ZIP writer's test reads its stored entries back with a few lines of its own.

## Hexframe consumer adapter and art

Hexframe stays a consumer; RigLab hands it the shapes it already loads (`src/hexframe/adapter.ts`).

- `toHexframeRig` returns the rig in Hexframe's rig file shape, with no format header, limits or chains, and its depth profiles; Hexframe's own `createRig` loads it. Hexframe looks a clip's profile up by its key alone, so the export also maps each animation's clip key to its class's profile, where the rig has one for the class and none for the clip: `sword-attack` paints `both-front` as `sword` does. The Export panel's rig download maps every animation. Hexframe paints by depth profile, so a rig without depth profiles is refused, and the left-out limits and chains are reported.
- `toHexframeClip` writes the clip RigLab's engine playback already plays (`engineOf`): the animation's 13 poses, constraints held (the class weapon's grips included), over `duration` = `length` ticks, `linear` between poses, with Hexframe's `key` and `note`. Hexframe's `sampleClip` plays it exactly as RigLab previews and bakes it, to 1e-9 on every frame. Hexframe's clip has no weapon channel, so the Export panel says that the weapon transforms are left out (`clipNotes`); the baked frames carry them.

Art attaches at slots, not the other way round. `src/hexframe/parts.ts` parses part SVGs (the chibi parts) as data: one `<svg>` root naming its bone, holding only `<path class d/>` elements with conservative values. Anything else (scripts, other elements or attributes, `url()` data) is refused, and nothing is injected as markup. `src/hexframe/art.ts` places each bone's part, found by the bone's `slot`, at that bone's FK transform, in Hexframe's paint order for facing right when the rig's depth profiles still fit it, and in bone order otherwise. Hexframe's own `depthProfileName(rig, animation, class)` picks the profile: the animation's `byClip` entry, else its class's, else the default. The editor's art layer passes the active animation and its class, and the wardrobe's figure the weapon it shows, else the motion's class. The chibi's default `anatomical` profile paints the far arm (`arm-front`, facing right) behind the torso. A two-handed weapon puts that hand on a grip in front of the body, so the chibi maps `sword`, `spear` and `axe` to `both-front`, which paints both arms over the torso. The daggers, which each ride one hand, keep the default. A held weapon's parts draw at each body's transform: `withWeaponArt` paints each body just under the last-painted lower bone of the chains that grip it, so the near hand covers a two-handed grip and each dagger sits under its own hand. Art is on when the editor opens, drawn under the skeleton in every mode, and the status bar's Art toggle hides it. The Export panel downloads the Hexframe rig and the active animation's clip.

## The chibi

The editor opens on the library's chibi, weapons and animations (`libraryModel` in `src/editor/model.ts`). The chibi is RigLab's only rig, and it comes from the library folder (see Library folders). The placeholder library holds one rest-pose unarmed animation per kind.

- **Chibi rig.** The library's `rig` line names the chibi rig, `rigs/chibi.rig.json` in both the owner's folder and `placeholder-library/`. It is a `riglab.rig` with Hexframe's 11 bone names, parents, chains and depth profiles at chibi proportions. Its elbows fold outward, away from the body. `arm-back` bends −1, as Hexframe's fighter does. `arm-front` bends +1 by the owner's direction on 2026-10-16, where the fighter's side view bends it −1. Hexframe never reads chains, because `toHexframeRig` leaves them out, so the bend sides only steer RigLab's solver. Its `byClip` maps the two-handed weapons to `both-front` (see the art paint order above). It stands 91 units tall, and the head bone is 44 of them, so it is about two heads tall. The pelvis sits 25 units up, the legs are 12 + 13 units, the arms 10 + 11, and both feet rest at y = 0. Its bones use their own `chibi_*` slots.
- **Chibi art.** The owner's library holds the 11 original part SVGs as skin `chibi`, in Hexframe's part format, read by the same strict parser. The style is MapleStory-like: a big round head with hair, bangs, big eyes and blush, a short tunic with a gold collar and belt, puffy sleeves, mitten hands, shorts and big shoes. It copies no MapleStory asset. Following Hexframe's default depth profile, which paints `arm-front` and `leg-front` behind the body when facing right, those limbs use the darker shades.
- **Skins.** The owner's library also holds the red-and-gold `armor`, `blue-mage` and `tier6-warlord` pixel skins from draft PR 15, each 11 parts of 2 × 2-unit cells with a pixel palette.
- **Art set.** The editor draws with the first skin in `config.txt`: `partsBySlot` (`src/library/parts.ts`) gives its parts on the rig's slots and every weapon's parts on theirs. Parts are keyed by slot and belong to their skin's art set, named after the skin. The art layer tags each part with its set, and `main.tsx` adds every skin's and weapon's palette, through `libraryCss`, as one stylesheet.

## Weapons

The library's `weapon` lines give the chibi's four weapons, each a directory with its `riglab.weapon` file, its part art, its palette and its first hold. The loaded library lists them in config order, the editor's model holds each one's rig and hold, and each draws in an art set named after it. The owner's parts are original, drawn in the strict part format, and their palette reuses the chibi's outline, gold, wood and cloth with a steel tone.

| Weapon | Bodies | Grips | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `sword` | One free body | `upper` (`arm-front`) and `lower` (`arm-back`), 7 apart on the hilt | Blade up from a gold crossguard, 51 units long |
| `spear` | One free body | `front` (`arm-front`) and `rear` (`arm-back`), 16 apart on the shaft | Leaf point toward +x, the facing direction, 70 units long |
| `axe` | One free body | `upper` (`arm-front`) and `lower` (`arm-back`), 10 apart on the haft | Crescent head up, its edge toward +x, 53 units long |
| `daggers` | `dagger-front` and `dagger-back`, one per hand | `fist` on `arm-front` and on `arm-back`, at 180° | Reverse grip: the blade leaves the pinky side of the fist and lies back along the forearm |

At chibi scale (91 units tall, arms 10 + 11, mitten fists about 7.6 units across) the grips fit two fists and stay reachable. Each weapon's `hold.json` gives its free bodies their first placement, in front of the chibi at the waist, tilted toward the facing side, so both hands reach their grips and the big head never hides the blade. `tests/weapons/` holds each two-handed weapon there with both feet planted and proves that both hands reach their grips without stretching. It also shows that in a raised guard each dagger's blade points down along its forearm.

## Library folders

A library folder holds a rig, its skins, the weapons it holds and every animation, the editor's only source of motion, mapped by a `config.txt` (`src/library/config.ts`). Each other line of `config.txt` is `<kind> <name> = <path>`, after blank lines and `#` comments are skipped. `kind` is `rig`, `skin`, `weapon` or `animation`, `name` is a RigLab name, and `path` is relative to the folder, with plain `/`-separated segments and no `..`. Line order is list order, and a library has exactly one rig.

- A **rig** line names a `riglab.rig` file whose `name` is the line's name.
- A **skin** line names a directory with one part SVG per rig slot, named after the slot without its first `_`-separated prefix (`chibi_arm_lower_l` → `arm_lower_l.svg`), whose `data-bone` is the bone that owns the slot. Next to the parts is a `palette.json` that defines every class token they use. A skin's art set is its config name. When the palette is `pixel`, every path must also keep the pixel-art contract (`src/library/pixel.ts`, [SVG pixel art](SVGPIXELART.md)): only separate `M{x} {y}h2v2h-2z` cells with even `x` and `y`, joined by single spaces.
- A **weapon** line names a directory with `weapon.json` (a `riglab.weapon` valid for the rig and named as the line is), one part SVG per body slot under the same naming rule, a `palette.json`, and a `hold.json` when the weapon has free bodies. Bodies may share a slot, as the daggers do, so a weapon part's `data-bone` is its slot's stem. No weapon may draw a slot the rig or another weapon draws.
- An **animation** line names a `riglab.anim` version 3 file for the rig, named as the line is, `<class>-<kind>`. Its class is `unarmed` or the name of a `weapon` line.

`loadLibrary(files)` (`src/library/load.ts`) takes folder-relative paths to file text and returns the rig, skins, weapons with their parts, palettes and holds, and animations, all in config order. Every part goes through the strict part parser. A failure lists every problem it found, each under its file or `config.txt` line. `placeholder-library/` is a library in this layout. Its rig and weapon rigs are the real ones and its art is plain boxes, so tests load it through the same path a real folder takes.

RigLab reads the folder named by `RIGLAB_LIBRARY`, else `~/Documents/RigLab/` when it holds a `config.txt`, else `placeholder-library/` (`src/library/folder.ts`). It reads `config.txt` and only what it maps: rig and animation files, and the `.txt`, `.json` and `.svg` files directly inside each skin and weapon directory. It refuses any path that resolves outside the folder, symlinks included, a mapped file of another type, and any file over 2 MiB. The Vite plugin (`src/library/plugin.ts`, loaded through `scripts/library-plugin.mjs`) serves the files as `virtual:riglab-library` to the dev server, the build and Vitest, after the loader accepts them; otherwise Vite's overlay lists every problem. Under `npm start` it watches the folder and reloads the page when a mapped file or `config.txt` changes. Vite restarts only for files its own config loader read, such as `vite.config.ts` and `scripts/library-plugin.mjs`, and that shim imports the plugin through Vite's `runnerImport`. So the shim hands the plugin the files it came from: the plugin's own file, which `runnerImport`'s `dependencies` leave out, and every module it imports. On the dev server, a change to one of them logs the files and restarts the server `RESTART_DELAY_MS` after the last change, so a burst restarts it once, and the restarted server imports the plugin afresh. A restart whose new code fails to load keeps the running server, plugin and watch. Vitest runs Vite in middleware mode and never restarts it. Vitest and `npm run check` (which builds with `--mode placeholder`) always read the placeholder, so they behave the same on every machine. `src/library/active.ts` loads the library once for the app, and the toolbar names it once, home-relative, or says it is the placeholder, ending in an ellipsis when the bar is too narrow. `npm run check:library [folder]` validates a folder outside `check`.

The dev server also writes the folder (`src/library/save.ts`, `src/library/write.ts`, `src/library/endpoint.ts`), so it is the single source of the rig, the weapon rigs and holds, and every animation:

- **Plan.** `planLibrarySave(files, { rig, weapons, animations })` puts the rig at the path its `rig` line maps, and each animation at its mapped path, or at `animations/<name>.anim.json` when it is new. `weapons` lists every library weapon in config order, each `{ weapon, hold }`: the plan writes its `weapon.json` in the directory its `weapon` line maps, and a weapon with free bodies its `hold.json` too, with the `riglab.hold` header. A weapon whose bodies all ride a hand sends a null hold, and a hold for it is refused. So is a weapon that no `weapon` line maps, one named unlike its line, one out of config order, and a missing one, because a save never adds, renames, moves or removes a weapon. It never writes a part SVG or a palette, or a `weapon` line. Only files whose text changes are written, so an unchanged weapon writes nothing. It rewrites the `animation` lines in the save's order where the first one stood, and leaves every other line and comment as it was. A renamed or deleted animation's old file leaves. A path another entry maps is refused. So is any plan whose merged files the loader would not accept, such as a rig edit that leaves a skin without a part. A refused plan writes nothing.
- **Write.** `writeLibrarySave` copies the previous version of every file it replaces or removes into `backups/<UTC stamp>/`. It writes each file through a temporary file and a rename, with `config.txt` last, and removes old files after that, so the config never maps a missing file. Every path, symlinks included, must stay inside the folder's real path.
- **Endpoint.** `POST /__riglab/library` exists only on the dev server. It accepts only a JSON body of at most 2 MiB from RigLab's own page on a loopback host: the `Origin` must equal `http://<Host>`, and `Sec-Fetch-Site`, when sent, must be `same-origin`. Vite's host check runs after plugin middleware, so the endpoint checks the host itself, against both cross-site pages and DNS-rebound names. It re-reads the folder for every save. The placeholder library is read-only.
- **Reloads.** After a save the plugin sends `riglab:library-saved` with the save's ID and the changed paths. The watcher ignores the files RigLab just wrote, its backups and its temporary files for a few seconds. An edit made outside RigLab still reloads every page.

## Wardrobe

`/wardrobe/` (`wardrobe/index.html`, `src/wardrobe/`) is a second page that steps through the library: the editor's toolbar links to it and it links back. It draws the library's rig large and read-only, with the art layer and no handles. Skin puts one skin on every slot. Each slot has its own control that cycles its part through every skin and shows which skin it came from, so parts mix and match. Class cycles Unarmed and each library weapon's class, and Motion cycles Rest and the five kinds, so switching class keeps the kind. A kind plays the class's animation of it, looped on Hexframe's clock through the engine, so skins can be checked at turned joints; one the library lacks shows as not in the library, at rest. At Rest an armed class holds its weapon at its `hold.json` placement, with the hands solved to its grips. ← and → step skins, ↑ and ↓ step slots, and `[` and `]` cycle the selected slot's part. Reset puts the current skin back on every slot. The outfit lives only in the page's pure state (`src/wardrobe/state.ts`): it is not saved and does not reach the editor. `npm run build` emits both pages. It reloads when the editor saves the library folder (`riglab:library-saved`), so it plays what was just saved.

## Acceptance

`tests/acceptance/spear-thrust.test.ts` drives the editor's own reducer through the RigLab recommendation's first acceptance path. It starts the chibi and lengthens both forearms in Rig setup. It then authors the spear class's attack (keys at 0, 8, 16 and 24), which `startClassAnimation` starts from the unarmed attack with the spear at its hold, as a thrust in Animate. At each key both feet are pinned, and the spear's move handle carries it through a wind-up with the hands drawn in and both elbows out, a linear transition, an extension with the hands pushed forward, and the recovery. It exports the bundle with baked frames and checks what export wrote: every file matches its manifest SHA-256, the written rig, animations and weapon equal the authored model's, and each baked file equals a fresh bake of them. On every frame, every joint must sit at its offset from its parent and every tip must keep its length (to 1e-5), both feet must stay on their pins, playback of the written animation must equal the original, and Hexframe's own sampler on the exported clip must place every bone where RigLab baked it. Both hands must hold the spear's grips at Hexframe's 13 poses (to 1e-5). Breaking connectivity by 0.01 units, or letting a non-root bone translate, fails it.

`tests/acceptance/weapons.test.ts` runs the same path once per weapon. In the weapon's class attack, started from the unarmed attack by `startClassAnimation`, the chibi plants both feet at each of the four keys and swings the weapon with the canvas handles in Animate: a free body by its move and turn handles, the daggers by dragging both hands. Each run checks that every key holds every pin and grip, exports with baked frames, and checks what export wrote as the spear thrust does. On every frame the skeleton must stay connected with every length kept, both feet planted, and Hexframe's sampler placing every bone where RigLab baked it. Each dagger must sit on its hand along its forearm on every frame (to 1e-5). A free body's grips must sit on their hands at Hexframe's 13 poses (to 1e-5) and within half a unit between them, where Hexframe interpolates joint angles rather than grips; the measured drift is at most 0.29 units.

The application is a React and TypeScript client rendered to native SVG and served by Vite. It needs no server, account, credential or native toolchain; `npm start` runs it locally. The repository contract keeps it that way: it rejects Rust, Cargo and Tauri files, `@tauri-apps` dependencies and package scripts that run `tauri`, `cargo`, `rustc` or `rustup`.
