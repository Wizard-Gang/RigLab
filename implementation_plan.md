### RL-048 — [FIX] Stop reporting a straightened limb as an error

- Dependency: None.
- Why: The owner reported on 2026-10-16: "the extension errors dont make sense. im able to pose it that way it shouldnt be an error. the legs are a big offender here". When a hand or foot is dragged past the limb's full length, `solveChain` straightens the limb toward the pointer, which is a valid pose. It still reports "<chain> cannot reach: the target is N beyond full extension", and the status bar shows that in red during the drag and again after the drop writes the key. No key in the owner's folder pins a foot, so every leg error comes from dragging a foot.
- Scope: A drag of a limb's own end reports nothing when the limb straightens or folds toward the pointer. A pin or weapon grip that a pose cannot hold becomes a plain note in the status bar's normal tone, for example "leg-front is 1.2 off its pin" or "arm-front is 2.0 off the sword's upper grip". That applies during a drag, after the drop, on a played frame and in Rig setup's weapon edits. Red stays for refused edits. The solver keeps its statuses for grid snapping and holds. Update README and ARCHITECTURE (Posing).
- Non-goals: Joint limits, IK behavior, the pin strain line on the canvas, and pin and grip semantics.
- Acceptance: Unit tests cover three cases. Dragging the `shin-front` tip 10 units past full extension straightens `leg-front` toward the target with no message. Moving the pelvis until a pinned foot leaves its pin gives the plain note in the info tone. A pose edit with that note writes the key with an info status. Headless Chrome on a worktree dev server drags a foot far below the ground, and the status bar never shows the error tone. `npm run check` passes.

### RL-050 — [FIX] Keep every bone's rotation continuous between Hexframe's poses

- Dependency: None.
- Why: The owner reported on 2026-10-16 that "swords are clipping weird" and asked for a full visual inspection. The inspection plays every frame with the editor's own engine. In the sword, axe and spear jumps, `arm-front` is 67.9° in one of Hexframe's 13 poses and -292.1° in the next. That is the same angle, but Hexframe's sampler interpolates the raw numbers, so the arm turns a full circle within three frames and both hands leave the weapon by up to 23 units. This happens in frames 16–17 and 31–32 of the sword and axe jumps and frames 19–20 and 28–29 of the spear jump. Export plays through the same engine, so baked clips carry the spin. The two-bone solver picks the angle nearest the keyed one, and the armed keys store angles a turn apart.
- Scope: `engineOf` unwraps each bone's rotation against the previous pose, so consecutive poses never differ by more than half a turn. RigLab's key sampler interpolates rotations by the shorter way. Rotations written to a key are kept within (-180, 180]. Update ARCHITECTURE (Playback).
- Non-goals: Changing Hexframe's sampler or the vendor pin, easing, the 13-pose grid, and deliberate spins of more than half a turn between two keys.
- Acceptance: Unit tests cover three cases. An animation whose keys store a bone at 67.9° and -292.1° plays every frame without the hands leaving a two-grip weapon by more than the existing 0.3-unit drift. The engine's consecutive poses differ by at most 180° for every bone. Key writes are wrapped. The frame audit of the owner's 25 animations finds no grip miss over 0.5 units. The bundle of the placeholder library keeps its SHA-256 from main unless a test shows why it changes. `npm run check` passes.
- Validation: Pinned `npm ci`, focused engine, sample and export tests, `npm run check`, `npm run audit:dependencies`, `check:patch`, `git diff --check`, the frame audit on a copy of the owner's folder, and exact-head CI.
- Authorities: `src/solve/engine.ts`, `src/format/sample.ts`, `src/editor/poseEdits.ts`, `src/solve/angles.ts`, `vendor/hexframe/src/rig/sample.ts`, `src/export/bake.ts`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, and the owner's report on 2026-10-16.

- Scope: `withWeaponArt` paints every held weapon body after the whole figure, the head included, and then the forearms that grip it, so the fists close over the grip. The editor, the wardrobe and baked art share the rule. In the owner's folder, after a backup, re-pose the jump. The airborne key raises the unarmed and dagger hands up and out beside the head instead of behind it. Every class's jump keeps both ankles on the ground through the crouch. Update the weapon-art tests and ARCHITECTURE (Art paint order).
